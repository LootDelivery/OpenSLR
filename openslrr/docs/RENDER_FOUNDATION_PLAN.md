# Render foundation plan

Status: plan only. Nothing in this document is implemented yet, and nothing here
authorises a change to what the frame draws without an agreed decision on the
stage in question.

## 1. Why this plan exists

The game looks simple and runs at 160-230 fps on a 4070 Ti in a city with traffic.
That is not a content problem, it is an architecture problem: the frame renders
the same city more than once, submits thousands of tiny draw calls, and encodes
all of them on the main thread. Future work (PBR, real lighting, shadows, a light
system) adds cost on top of that shape unless the shape changes first.

This plan fixes the shape in an order where each stage pays for itself: reflection
probes remove a whole scene pass *and* are the substrate PBR needs, materials
sorting and instancing cut the draw count, and only then is it worth spreading the
work over threads.

## 2. Where the frame goes now

Measured with the shipped profiler (`--perf-log -`), the traffic stage profiler
(`SLRR_TRAFFIC_PROFILE=1`) and the stack sampler (`SLRR_STACK_SAMPLE=1`). Parked
camera in ValoCity, 2560x1440, no vsync.

Frame with 100 ambient cars and pedestrians at maximum:

| Zone | ms | Note |
|---|---|---|
| `flush` (whole main-thread render) | 4.8 | |
|  `collect` (scene walk) | 1.3 | |
|  `refl` (cubemap capture) | 0.9 | |
|  `world` (props + vehicles + pedestrians) | 1.1 | |
|  `shadows` | 0.1 | |
| `traffic` (tick) | 0.7…0.85 | |
| `physics` | 0.35 | |
| `scripts` (VM) | 0.3 | |
| `present` (wait for the render thread) | 1.0…1.3 | |
| **frame** | **6.5** | |
| `gpu` | **6.3** | both CPU and GPU are at the wall |

The traffic tick decomposes further at 100 cars: `park` 0.416, `drive` without
`park` 0.208, `floor` 0.079, `wheels` 0.050, `pose` 0.036, `retire` 0.032 ms.
`park` is `gameref_set_matrix` + `part_park_chassis` per car, ~4.2 us per car per
frame, paid for every car in the band including those out of view.

Traffic's cost outside the tick, from 20 to 100 cars: draws 3458 -> 5316
(+23 per car), `flush` +0.93 ms, `collect` +0.32 ms, `physics` +0.09 ms.

The reflection cubemap, measured by freezing the capture after the first cube
(`SLRR_REFL_FREEZE=1`), on a city with no traffic spawned:

| | draws | `flush` | `gpu` |
|---|---|---|---|
| capture every frame | 2995 | 2.15 ms | 2.62 ms |
| capture frozen | 1860 | 1.36 ms | 1.85 ms |

So the cube is a second full render of the static city, every frame: +1135 draws,
+0.79 ms of main thread, +0.77 ms of GPU. It does not scale with traffic today
only because cars are excluded from the capture.

## 3. Reflection probes

### 3.1 What exists

`update_reflections` renders the scene into a 512x512 RGBA8 cube, six faces, once
per frame, far plane 150 m, probe origin at the camera eye + 0.8 m, two cubes
double-buffered so the material never samples the cube being written, mips
generated by the backend when the last face resolves.

The vehicle shader samples it with a mip level chosen from roughness:

```glsl
float lod = clamp(radius * 14.0, 0.0, 4.0);
return textureCubeLod(s_sceneReflection, dir, lod).rgb;
```

That is a mip pick over a driver-generated box-filtered chain, not a physical
prefilter. Parallax correction already exists (`u_reflectionProbe`,
`u_reflectionBoxMin/Max`), so the concept of a probe is half present: the code has
a position, a box and a parallax path, but only one implicit probe, on the camera.

`fs_vehicle.sc` also declares `s_sceneReflection1..4`, which nothing samples. They
are leftovers from an earlier multi-level prefilter attempt: writing a non-zero
mip of a cube through a blit did not take effect, so each blur level was put in
its own cube at mip 0. Any new prefilter has to respect that finding.

### 3.2 What "prefiltered cubemap" means

A cube where each mip level stores the environment convolved with a GGX lobe of a
given roughness (importance sampling, typically 64-1024 samples per texel), built
when the probe is baked rather than when it is sampled. The shader then does one
`textureCubeLod(dir, roughness -> lod)`, and the result is physically plausible:
a mirror at roughness 0, soft highlights at 0.5, a matte haze at 1.

This is the specular half of image based lighting (the split-sum approximation).
The diffuse half is irradiance: a 32x32 cube or nine spherical harmonic
coefficients. The frame has neither today: ambient is the flat `u_sunAmb` uniform,
which is the first thing a PBR material will expose.

### 3.3 New requirement: cars in the reflections

Today the capture skips every object with `vehicle_material`, so the cube contains
no cars at all. That was done so the player's car does not reflect itself, but it
is wrong for the rest of the traffic: a car in a city should see the other cars
reflected in its paint and chrome.

Required policy:

1. the car the material belongs to must never appear in its own reflection;
2. every other car must appear;
3. the driver render object stays out (already keyed separately);
4. the policy must be per-probe, because a baked static probe of a street block
   has no cars in it by construction.

This changes the cost model: cars in the cube are extra draws, bounded by the
150 m far plane and by the frustum of each face. It has to be measured before it
is switched on, and it argues against a purely static bake as the whole answer.

### 3.4 Variants

**A. Baked static probe, refreshed on load and on time-of-day change.**
Builds the GGX chain once from the static city and sky. Cost in the frame: zero.
Picture: paint and chrome reflect the buildings exactly as they stood at bake time.
Pedestrians and (with the new requirement) other cars are missing, so this alone
does not satisfy 3.3. Best used as the *static layer* of the answer.

**B. Dynamic capture, less often.** One or more faces per frame, whole cube every
N frames. Cost divides by N. Picture: reflections lag by up to N/60 s; invisible
on paint, visible on chrome and mirrors at speed. Satisfies 3.3 naturally, because
it is the current capture with a throttle.

**C. Hybrid: per-block static probes plus a dynamic layer.** Several baked probes
placed in the city (a probe per street block or per cell), selected per object by
the parallax box that already exists, plus a small dynamic capture that contains
only what moves: cars and pedestrians. The shader samples the static cube for the
environment and the dynamic cube for the moving objects, or the two are composited
into the dynamic cube with the static environment as its background.

This is the end state for PBR: probes become data (position, box, cubes, bake
state), which is exactly what an IBL material needs, and it is the only variant
that satisfies 3.3 without paying for a full scene capture every frame.

Cost model for C, to be confirmed by measurement: one baked probe set (512 with a
full mip chain, RGBA8) is about 8 MB; the dynamic layer can be 128-256 with a
short chain because it holds only cars; the dynamic capture draws cars only,
which at 100 cars in 150 m is a few hundred draws instead of the current 1135.

Recommendation: measure stage 0 first, then build A (it is the substrate, and it
removes the pass), then move to C (adds the dynamic layer and per-block probes).
B is a stopgap that can be evaluated on the same measurements but does not build
the substrate.

## 4. Stages

Each stage is its own block with its own measurement and commit, in this order.
Stages 3 and 4 are worth nothing until the GPU ceiling moves, so they come after.

| Stage | Deliverable | Acceptance |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Measurement: GPU cost per pass (main view, cubemap, shadows), density sweep 0 / 0.2 / 0.5 / 1.0 on one camera; per-probe capture cost with cars included and excluded | A table of pass -> ms and draws -> fps for this machine, recorded in this document |
| 1 | Probes as data: probe struct, registry, selection per object, bake scheduler; GGX prefilter built into the probe's mip chain (or into separate cubes if the blit limitation still holds); irradiance for diffuse ambient | Paint, chrome and glass look unchanged or better in A/B screenshots; the per-frame capture pass is gone or reduced; PBR can read a prefiltered cube and irradiance |
| 2 | Materials: sort draws by material and texture, then instance identical materials with a persistent instance buffer (not a transient one) | Draw count per car drops; GPU time per car drops; cars still render exactly as before |
| 3 | Parallel command encoding: one `bgfx::Encoder` per worker, instance ranges split by owner, the transparent pass ordered explicitly | `world` zone drops, frame time follows if the GPU allows |
| 4 | Parallel scene walk: `collect` split by owner or cell, read-only against the reference map during the walk | `collect` zone drops |
| 5 | Shadows and lights: tighter shadow casters, cascaded or budgeted shadow map, a real light list | After PBR is decided |

## 5. Constraints and lessons

1. **Do not throttle reflection capture for cost.** Tried in `60d7bb5f`, reverted
   in `093376d5`. The six faces are the cube; halving the cadence changes what the
   paint and glass reflect.
2. **Do not cache material uniform binding across draws.** Tried in the same
   commit: doors and the hood lost their reflections while the glass kept them.
   The env filter, the probe and its box are material inputs, not frame state.
3. **Any instanced path must bound its instance data allocation** with
   `bgfx::getAvailInstanceDataBuffer` and chunk the group. `allocInstanceDataBuffer`
   clamps the request and still returns a pointer, so writing a whole group into
   the clamped slice corrupts the draws that follow: cars collapsed, lost their
   bodies and wheels, or left only a shadow (`aa92b08b`, reverted in `bcbc4f1b`).
4. **Instancing for its own sake lost on the GPU** in the naive form: 1350 -> 615
   vehicle submits, but 3.59 -> 5.77 ms of GPU. Stage 2 must be measured per
   material group, not assumed from the draw count.
5. Nothing that changes what the screen shows is merged without an agreed plan and
   a before/after visual check on paint, chrome, glass and the body panels.

## 6. Open questions

1. Is a per-block static probe acceptable as the environment (buildings frozen at
   bake time), with cars and pedestrians in a separate dynamic layer? That is the
   shape of variant C.
2. How much lag is acceptable on chrome? It decides whether the dynamic layer is
   captured every frame, every second frame, or on demand.
3. Do future materials need per-object probe selection, or is one probe per ground
   enough?
4. Is the PBR block expected to arrive with its own material format (texture set,
   parameters), so that stage 2 sorts on a stable material key rather than on the
   legacy SCX material types?

## 7. Measurement plan for stage 0

All of these run on the shipped build with existing knobs; none of them changes
code.

| Run | Knobs | What it answers |
|---|---|---|
| Pass split | default vs `SLRR_REFL_FREEZE=1` vs `SLRR_REFL_RES=128` | cost of the cubemap at full and reduced resolution |
| Shadows | `Vehicle shadows` / `Map shadows` toggled in the debug overlay | cost of the shadow pass |
| Density sweep | `SLRR_TRAFFIC_DENSITY` 0 / 0.2 / 0.5 / 1.0, pedestrians 0, `SLRR_TRAFFIC_FREEZE=1` | draws and frame time per population, marginal cost per car |
| Vehicles in the cube | requires the policy change, measured in stage 1 | what requirement 3.3 costs |

Because run to run variance in this scene is 5-10 %, each comparison needs the
same camera, the same population and a mean over at least 30 one-second samples;
`--bench-drive 0` parks the camera and freezes the population is the closest thing
to a controlled scene, and `--bench-route` is not usable until the autopilot stops
flipping the car.

## 8. Stage 0 results (measured)

ValoCity, parked camera, 100 ambient cars and pedestrians at maximum, mean of 35
one-second samples per row, Release build:

| Cube | draws | `flush` | `refl` | `gpu` | frame | fps |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 512 as shipped | 5264 | 2.90 | 0.75 | 2.94 | 4.18 | 241 |
| frozen (capture skipped) | 3556 | 1.91 | 0.00 | 1.81 | 3.14 | 321 |
| 128 | 4857 | 2.62 | 0.69 | 2.37 | 3.88 | 259 |

The conclusion changes the design: **the capture is draw bound, not pixel
bound.** Dropping the cube from 512 to 128 removes a quarter of the pixels and
returns only 0.56 ms of GPU, while skipping the capture returns 1.13 ms and 1708
draws. The cost is the per-face culling and submission of the city, six times per
frame.

What follows for the probes:

1. A baked static probe is the only variant that removes the whole cost, because
   it removes the draws rather than making them cheaper.
2. A dynamic layer must keep a small radius. Cars are currently excluded; with
the policy in 3.3 they come in, and at 23 draws per car a 150 m radius would add
   roughly what the city already costs. A 40-60 m layer drawn into a 128-256 cube
   keeps it to a few hundred draws.
3. Compositing the dynamic layer over the static environment as its background
   keeps the shader at one cube fetch, so the hybrid does not pay twice per pixel.

Still to measure: how many cars fall inside a 40 m and a 60 m radius around the
player in city traffic, which sets the size of the dynamic layer; and the shadow
pass cost, which needs the overlay toggles.
