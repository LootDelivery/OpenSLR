# Render foundation plan

Status: plan only. Nothing in this document is implemented yet, and nothing here
authorises a change to what the frame draws without an agreed decision on the
stage in question.

## 1. Why this plan exists

The game looks simple and runs at 160-230 fps on a 4070 Ti in a city with traffic.
That is not a content problem, it is an architecture problem: the frame renders
the same city more than once, submits thousands of tiny draw calls, and encodes
all of them on the main thread. Future work (PBR, real lighting, shadows, a light
system) adds cost on top of that shape unless the shape changes first.

This plan fixes the shape in an order where each stage pays for itself: reflection
probes remove a whole scene pass *and* are the substrate PBR needs, materials
sorting and instancing cut the draw count, and only then is it worth spreading the
work over threads.

So the cube is a second full render of the static city, every frame: +1135 draws,
+0.79 ms of main thread, +0.77 ms of GPU. It does not scale with traffic today
only because cars are excluded from the capture.

## 3. Reflection probes

### 3.1 What exists

`update_reflections` renders the scene into a 512x512 RGBA8 cube, six faces, once
per frame, far plane 150 m, probe origin at the camera eye + 0.8 m, two cubes
double-buffered so the material never samples the cube being written, mips
generated by the backend when the last face resolves.

The vehicle shader samples it with a mip level chosen from roughness:

```glsl
float lod = clamp(radius * 14.0, 0.0, 4.0);
return textureCubeLod(s_sceneReflection, dir, lod).rgb;
```

That is a mip pick over a driver-generated box-filtered chain, not a physical
prefilter. Parallax correction already exists (`u_reflectionProbe`,
`u_reflectionBoxMin/Max`), so the concept of a probe is half present: the code has
a position, a box and a parallax path, but only one implicit probe, on the camera.

`fs_vehicle.sc` also declares `s_sceneReflection1..4`, which nothing samples. They
are leftovers from an earlier multi-level prefilter attempt: writing a non-zero
mip of a cube through a blit did not take effect, so each blur level was put in
its own cube at mip 0. Any new prefilter has to respect that finding.

### 3.2 What "prefiltered cubemap" means

A cube where each mip level stores the environment convolved with a GGX lobe of a
given roughness (importance sampling, typically 64-1024 samples per texel), built
when the probe is baked rather than when it is sampled. The shader then does one
`textureCubeLod(dir, roughness -> lod)`, and the result is physically plausible:
a mirror at roughness 0, soft highlights at 0.5, a matte haze at 1.

This is the specular half of image based lighting (the split-sum approximation).
The diffuse half is irradiance: a 32x32 cube or nine spherical harmonic
coefficients. The frame has neither today: ambient is the flat `u_sunAmb` uniform,
which is the first thing a PBR material will expose.

### 3.3 New requirement: cars in the reflections

Today the capture skips every object with `vehicle_material`, so the cube contains
no cars at all. That was done so the player's car does not reflect itself, but it
is wrong for the rest of the traffic: a car in a city should see the other cars
reflected in its paint and chrome.

Required policy:

1. the car the material belongs to must never appear in its own reflection;
2. every other car must appear;
3. the driver render object stays out (already keyed separately);
4. the policy must be per-probe, because a baked static probe of a street block
   has no cars in it by construction.

This changes the cost model: cars in the cube are extra draws, bounded by the
150 m far plane and by the frustum of each face. It has to be measured before it
is switched on, and it argues against a purely static bake as the whole answer.

### 3.4 Variants

**A. Baked static probe, refreshed on load and on time-of-day change.**
Builds the GGX chain once from the static city and sky. Cost in the frame: zero.
Picture: paint and chrome reflect the buildings exactly as they stood at bake time.
Pedestrians and (with the new requirement) other cars are missing, so this alone
does not satisfy 3.3. Best used as the *static layer* of the answer.

**B. Dynamic capture, less often.** One or more faces per frame, whole cube every
N frames. Cost divides by N. Picture: reflections lag by up to N/60 s; invisible
on paint, visible on chrome and mirrors at speed. Satisfies 3.3 naturally, because
it is the current capture with a throttle.

**C. Hybrid: per-block static probes plus a dynamic layer.** Several baked probes
placed in the city (a probe per street block or per cell), selected per object by
the parallax box that already exists, plus a small dynamic capture that contains
only what moves: cars and pedestrians. The shader samples the static cube for the
environment and the dynamic cube for the moving objects, or the two are composited
into the dynamic cube with the static environment as its background.

This is the end state for PBR: probes become data (position, box, cubes, bake
state), which is exactly what an IBL material needs, and it is the only variant
that satisfies 3.3 without paying for a full scene capture every frame.

Cost model for C, to be confirmed by measurement: one baked probe set (512 with a
full mip chain, RGBA8) is about 8 MB; the dynamic layer can be 128-256 with a
short chain because it holds only cars; the dynamic capture draws cars only,
which at 100 cars in 150 m is a few hundred draws instead of the current 1135.

Recommendation: measure stage 0 first, then build A (it is the substrate, and it
removes the pass), then move to C (adds the dynamic layer and per-block probes).
B is a stopgap that can be evaluated on the same measurements but does not build
the substrate.

## 4. Stages

Each stage is its own block with its own measurement and commit, in this order.
Stages 3 and 4 are worth nothing until the GPU ceiling moves, so they come after.

| Stage | Deliverable | Acceptance |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Measurement: GPU cost per pass (main view, cubemap, shadows), density sweep 0 / 0.2 / 0.5 / 1.0 on one camera; per-probe capture cost with cars included and excluded | A table of pass -> ms and draws -> fps for this machine, recorded in this document |
| 1 | Probes as data: probe struct, registry, selection per object, bake scheduler; GGX prefilter built into the probe's mip chain (or into separate cubes if the blit limitation still holds); irradiance for diffuse ambient | Paint, chrome and glass look unchanged or better in A/B screenshots; the per-frame capture pass is gone or reduced; PBR can read a prefiltered cube and irradiance |
| 1a | **Done** (`b407dbd6`): the static environment is baked into its own double-buffered cube, one face per frame, redone on world change, on 25 m of movement and on a 240 frame refresh; each frame a blit copies the bake into the capture face and only the moving objects are drawn over it, so a material still samples one cube | See 8.4 |
| 2 | Materials: sort draws by material and texture, then instance identical materials with a persistent instance buffer (not a transient one) | Draw count per car drops; GPU time per car drops; cars still render exactly as before |
| 3 | Parallel command encoding: one `bgfx::Encoder` per worker, instance ranges split by owner, the transparent pass ordered explicitly | `world` zone drops, frame time follows if the GPU allows |
| 4 | Parallel scene walk: `collect` split by owner or cell, read-only against the reference map during the walk | `collect` zone drops |
| 5 | Shadows and lights: tighter shadow casters, cascaded or budgeted shadow map, a real light list | After PBR is decided |

## 5. Constraints and lessons

1. **Do not throttle reflection capture for cost.** Tried in `60d7bb5f`, reverted
   in `093376d5`. The six faces are the cube; halving the cadence changes what the
   paint and glass reflect.
2. **Do not cache material uniform binding across draws.** Tried in the same
   commit: doors and the hood lost their reflections while the glass kept them.
   The env filter, the probe and its box are material inputs, not frame state.
3. **Any instanced path must bound its instance data allocation** with
   `bgfx::getAvailInstanceDataBuffer` and chunk the group. `allocInstanceDataBuffer`
   clamps the request and still returns a pointer, so writing a whole group into
   the clamped slice corrupts the draws that follow: cars collapsed, lost their
   bodies and wheels, or left only a shadow (`aa92b08b`, reverted in `bcbc4f1b`).
4. **Instancing for its own sake lost on the GPU** in the naive form: 1350 -> 615
   vehicle submits, but 3.59 -> 5.77 ms of GPU. Stage 2 must be measured per
   material group, not assumed from the draw count.
5. Nothing that changes what the screen shows is merged without an agreed plan and
   a before/after visual check on paint, chrome, glass and the body panels.

## 6. Open questions

1. Is a per-block static probe acceptable as the environment (buildings frozen at
   bake time), with cars and pedestrians in a separate dynamic layer? That is the
   shape of variant C.
2. How much lag is acceptable on chrome? It decides whether the dynamic layer is
   captured every frame, every second frame, or on demand.
3. Do future materials need per-object probe selection, or is one probe per ground
   enough?
4. Is the PBR block expected to arrive with its own material format (texture set,
   parameters), so that stage 2 sorts on a stable material key rather than on the
   legacy SCX material types?

## 7. Measurement plan for stage 0

All of these run on the shipped build with existing knobs; none of them changes
code.

| Run | Knobs | What it answers |
|---|---|---|
| Pass split | default vs `SLRR_REFL_FREEZE=1` vs `SLRR_REFL_RES=128` | cost of the cubemap at full and reduced resolution |
| Shadows | `Vehicle shadows` / `Map shadows` toggled in the debug overlay | cost of the shadow pass |
| Density sweep | `SLRR_TRAFFIC_DENSITY` 0 / 0.2 / 0.5 / 1.0, pedestrians 0, `SLRR_TRAFFIC_FREEZE=1` | draws and frame time per population, marginal cost per car |
| Vehicles in the cube | requires the policy change, measured in stage 1 | what requirement 3.3 costs |

Because run to run variance in this scene is 5-10 %, each comparison needs the
same camera, the same population and a mean over at least 30 one-second samples;
`--bench-drive 0` parks the camera and freezes the population is the closest thing
to a controlled scene, and `--bench-route` is not usable until the autopilot stops
flipping the car.

## 8. Stage 0 results (measured)

ValoCity, parked camera, 100 ambient cars and pedestrians at maximum, mean of 35
one-second samples per row, Release build:

| Cube | draws | `flush` | `refl` | `gpu` | frame | fps |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 512 as shipped | 5264 | 2.90 | 0.75 | 2.94 | 4.18 | 241 |
| frozen (capture skipped) | 3556 | 1.91 | 0.00 | 1.81 | 3.14 | 321 |
| 128 | 4857 | 2.62 | 0.69 | 2.37 | 3.88 | 259 |

The conclusion changes the design: **the capture is draw bound, not pixel
bound.** Dropping the cube from 512 to 128 removes a quarter of the pixels and
returns only 0.56 ms of GPU, while skipping the capture returns 1.13 ms and 1708
draws. The cost is the per-face culling and submission of the city, six times per
frame.

What follows for the probes:

1. A baked static probe is the only variant that removes the whole cost, because
   it removes the draws rather than making them cheaper.
2. A dynamic layer must keep a small radius. Cars are currently excluded; with
the policy in 3.3 they come in, and at 23 draws per car a 150 m radius would add
   roughly what the city already costs. A 40-60 m layer drawn into a 128-256 cube
   keeps it to a few hundred draws.
3. Compositing the dynamic layer over the static environment as its background
   keeps the shader at one cube fetch, so the hybrid does not pay twice per pixel.

Still to measure: how many cars fall inside a 40 m and a 60 m radius around the
player in city traffic, which sets the size of the dynamic layer; and the shadow
pass cost, which needs the overlay toggles.

### 8.1 Density sweep and the population near the player

Same camera, pedestrians off, population frozen, mean of 35 one-second samples:

| density | cars | draws | `flush` | `world` | `gpu` | frame | fps |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.0 | 0 | 2925 | 2.14 | 0.45 | 2.65 | 2.66 | 376 |
| 0.2 | 20 | 3373 | 1.98 | 0.53 | 2.64 | 2.65 | 377 |
| 0.5 | 50 | 3997 | 2.04 | 0.58 | 2.78 | 2.93 | 342 |
| 1.0 | 100 | 5174 | 2.54 | 0.76 | 3.04 | 3.87 | 261 |

Marginal cost: 22.5 draws per car, about 0.3 ms of GPU for the step from 0 to 100
cars, and the frame only starts to follow above ~4000 draws. At 20 cars the scene
is not draw bound at all, which is why 0.0 and 0.2 measure the same.

The traffic log counts every visible car within 140 m (`near`) and within 340 m in
front (`ahead`). At density 1.0 in city traffic: **`near` = 10-12, `ahead` = 29-32,
`total` = 100**. So only ~11 cars sit inside a 140 m radius of the player.

That changes the estimate for 3.3: putting the other cars into the capture is
cheap. Eleven cars at 22.5 draws, each appearing in one or two of the six faces,
is roughly 250-400 draws, against the 1708 the static city already costs. The
policy "exclude the controlled car, include the rest" is affordable even with the
capture as it is today; the dynamic layer of the hybrid is a size question for
chrome quality, not for cost.

### 8.3 Cars in the capture: policy in, and what it exposed

The policy from 3.3 is implemented now: the capture skips only the controlled
chassis (falling back to the car nearest the probe within 6 m when nothing is
controlled) and draws every other car. Measured on the same camera, 100 cars and
pedestrians at maximum:

| | draws | `refl` | `gpu` | frame | fps |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| cars excluded (before) | 5264 | 0.75 | 2.94 | 4.18 | 241 |
| other cars included | 6739 | 1.48 | 3.35 | 4.81 | 208 |

The step cost 1475 draws, which is 65 car draws at 22.5 per car, while only about
eleven cars are within 140 m of the player. The reason was a gap in the collection
path: **`scene_instances_collect` does not cull ordinary objects against the
frustum it is handed.** The frustum only decides which walkers get a bone palette
and which static prop cells are drawn; every car, part and pedestrian in the world
is returned and drawn regardless of where the view points. Each of the six cube
faces therefore drew every nearby car.

Two follow-ups closed it, and the second is the largest cheap win found so far:

1. The car the probe belongs to was not being excluded at all, only its chassis.
   `part_render_owner` stops at the first installed record and every body part --
   door, hood, trunk -- is a record of its own, so the user could see his own boot
   lid in the paint. `part_render_car` walks on to the first record whose native
   kind is "car"; the capture now drops 94 objects per face for that car.
2. `scene_instance_in_frustum` is applied per face before anything is drawn. It is
   the same bounding-sphere test walkers already used, with a metre of slack.

Camera parked, 100 cars and pedestrians at maximum, medians of 35 samples:

| | draws | `refl` | `flush` | `gpu` | frame | fps |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| cars out, no cull | 5309 | 0.74 | 2.92 | 2.99 | 4.21 | 238 |
| cars in, no cull | 5812 | 1.24 | 3.58 | 3.54 | 4.95 | 202 |
| cars in, faces culled | 4133 | 0.65 | 2.49 | 2.78 | 3.83 | 261 |

The capture with traffic in it is now cheaper than the capture without traffic
was, and the frame is faster than before this work started. Objects drawn per face
fall from ~800 to ~55, and the counts stay directional: no cars at all in the up
and down faces.

One regression came out of the cull and is worth recording, because it is the
kind of thing a measurement does not catch. The asphalt vanished from the paint.
The ground and the road are drawn as one world-space mesh whose bounding box spans
the map and sits at the world origin, so against a 150 m face every plane rejects
it while the road is exactly what the face shows. Ground and sky are now exempt
from the sphere test, and the up face, the only face that cannot see the ground,
skips it.

Keeping the ground costs 2289 draws in the capture, which is most of what is left
in it. With traffic included and the faces culled, the capture is 6160 draws and
0.87 ms, and the frame is 5.03 ms instead of 4.21 ms before any of this work. So
traffic in the reflections costs 851 draws and about 0.8 ms on this camera; the
rest of the capture is the static world drawn five times, which is exactly what
the baked probe removes in stage 1.

What is left is the same gap in the **main view**, which still draws the cars
behind the player: the cost of traffic scales with the whole population instead of
with what is on screen, which is why moving the camera changed `draws` so little
in every measurement above. It cannot be pushed into `scene_instances_collect`,
because that list feeds the per-car shadow maps and a car just outside the view
still casts a shadow into it. It has to be a per-pass cull: view frustum for the
camera, and for the shadow pass either a light-space set or the same list widened
by the shadow radius.

### 8.2 Shadows today (reported by the user, confirmed in the code)

What exists is not a shadow system to optimise, it is three unrelated things:

| Source | How it is produced | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| map / trees | cached map shadow texture, `bgfx_shadows.cpp` | about 10 m around the object, no building shadows at all |
| player and rival cars | one shadow map per car: bodies with `(flags & 9) == 8` are drawn white into a small target and the ground is multiplied by it | only bodies that carry those flags |
| ambient traffic | flat `kocsiarnyek` decal quad, never a caster | a blob under the car |

The frame only spends 0.10 ms of main thread on `shadows`, so nothing here is
worth optimising. Stage 5 replaces it: building shadows need a real shadow map
with a cascade or a budget, traffic needs to cast instead of wearing a decal, and
the cached map texture goes away.

### 8.4 Stage 1a: the baked environment

The static world is no longer captured every frame. It goes into its own
double-buffered cube, one face per frame, and is redone only when the world
changes, when the probe has moved 25 m or on a slow refresh, so streamed content
still lands in it. Each frame a blit copies the bake into the capture face and
only the moving objects are drawn on top. A material therefore still samples one
cube and the shader did not change.

Camera parked in ValoCity, 100 cars and pedestrians at maximum, medians of 30
samples:

| | draws | `refl` | `flush` | `gpu` | frame |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| per-frame capture | 5049 | 0.46 | 2.63 | 2.67 | 3.90 |
| bake, moving layer every frame | 4309 | 0.34 | 2.58 | 2.23 | 3.87 |
| bake, moving layer every 2 frames | 4145 | 0.17 | 2.24 | 2.12 | 3.54 |

Switches, all measurement: `SLRR_REFL_BAKE=0` restores the per-frame capture,
`SLRR_REFL_DYN=0` keeps the bake and drops the moving layer, `SLRR_REFL_DYN_INTERVAL`
sets the moving layer cadence, `SLRR_REFL_BAKE_DIST` and `SLRR_REFL_BAKE_REFRESH`
set when the bake is redone.

The bake follows the camera by default: a face costs 0.15-0.25 ms of main thread,
so the cube is refreshed continuously. Its cadence is expressed in **milliseconds,
not frames**, and this is the one thing a measurement on an uncapped frame rate
hides completely. With vsync on, a frame is 16 ms, so a six-frame cycle is a tenth
of a second old and the user could see the reflections updating late; the same six
frames at 250 fps are 24 ms and look instant. The cycle now gets as many faces per
frame as it needs to stay under 32 ms: one at 250 fps, three at 144, four at 60.
The moving layer is gated the same way, at most every 16 ms. A distance threshold was tried first and the
user could see the reflections standing still between rebakes. Continuous costs
about 0.25 ms of frame time against the old per-frame capture and is still 0.25 ms
cheaper than it:

| | draws | `refl` | `gpu` | frame |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| per-frame capture | 4852 | 0.40 | 3.49 | 3.79 |
| bake follows the camera | 4309 | 0.29 | 3.21 | 3.56 |
| bake every 8 m | 4511 | 0.18 | 3.18 | 3.77 |

Two more traps from this step, both of the same kind -- a frame id or a frame
count shared between two passes that can now run in the same frame:

1. The bake and the moving layer were writing through the same view ids, so on a
   frame that did both the last `setViewFrameBuffer` won and the other face was
   never written. They now own separate ranges (`kReflectionBakeView`,
   `kReflectionCaptureView`).
2. The moving layer's cadence gate took a modulus of the frame number, which
   resonates with the six-frame bake cycle: the pass was rejected on every frame it
   could have run. It now counts frames since the last pass instead.

Three things this cost, all of them found by looking at the cube rather than at
the frame time, which is why `SLRR_REFL_DUMP=2` now forces the face panels on so a
bench run can photograph them:

1. A bake taken in the first frames is a cube with sky in it and nothing else: the
   props stream in after the load. The bake waits for
   `scene_instances_has_ground_props` and stays unready while it has no props, and
   the per-frame capture keeps running until it is ready.
2. Seeding the face by drawing `fs_cubeview` through `draw_fullscreen` produced a
   black face. `bgfx::blit` from bake face to capture face works, and has to run in
   its own view one below the capture view, because blits inside a view execute
   before that view's draws.
3. With the bake in place the moving layer is only vehicles, drivers and
   pedestrians over it, and the ray between those two halves is decided before the
   frustum cull so the counts in the trace stay readable.

Still open in stage 1: GGX prefiltering of the bake's mip chain and a separate
irradiance probe for diffuse ambient. Both are additive to this structure: the
cube already exists as an object with its own schedule, so they change how its mips
are built, not how it is captured.
### 8.5 Stage 1b: attempted, measured, reverted

The cube's mips are a box filter, so a roughness lookup picks a level blurred by
area: chrome does not care, semi-rough paint keeps the hard edges of whatever was
bright. A second cube convolved with GGX importance sampling -- one level per
frame, level `m` built at the angular width of `2^m` texels of the capture, sharp
reads left on the capture cube -- was written, compiled and measured. It cost
0.06 ms of a 3.6 ms frame.

It was also **inert**. Sampling mip 1 and mip 5 of the convolved cube from inside
the vehicle shader returns 0, and the same pass writing into a 16 texel cube for
the irradiance probe left the clear colour untouched. Four facts, taken together:

* the pass runs (a trace printed once per level and frame, 1384 times a run);
* the program is valid, the source cube is valid, the transient buffer is
  available, and the framebuffers report valid;
* the clear colour of the same view **does** land in the cube, so the target and
  the view are right and only the draw is missing;
* the capture and the bake, which draw the scene into the same kind of per-face
  cube framebuffer with `bgfx::submit`, work.

So the suspect is `draw_fullscreen` itself -- the full-screen quad helper the old
blur chain used, which is why that chain reads as dead code. Two candidates were
not settled before the work was pulled: the quad is submitted with
`bgfx::setViewTransform(view, nullptr, ortho)`, and the vertex data is a
`MeshVertex` filled by hand against a 52-byte layout with three UV sets and a
packed colour. Either can produce a draw that is submitted, costs nothing, and
rasterises nowhere.

Nothing of stage 1b ships: a cube whose convolved levels are black is worse than
box mips, and the `lod >= 1` branch would have handed those mips to every material
rough enough to ask for them. The lesson is the one from U2-B.4 in another form:
**measure the artefact, not the mechanism.** The pass had a trace, a valid handle
and a plausible cost, and none of that is evidence that a pixel arrived.

What to do first next time: prove `draw_fullscreen` renders at all -- draw a
magenta quad with it into the OSD view and look at the screen -- before building
anything on top of it.
### 8.6 Freshness: what the cube's age is made of

A reflection that trails a moving car is not a shading artefact, it is the age of
the cube. The age has three parts, and all three were counted in frames at first:

```
age  =  bake cycle  +  moving layer  +  prefilter cycle
```

The bake builds `clamp(ceil(6 * frame_ms / cycle_ms), 1, 6)` faces a frame, and
with `cycle_ms` at 32 and a 4 ms frame that is one face, so a whole cube every six
frames -- 24 ms at 250 fps, which is a visible trail. The moving layer waited 16 ms
on top of it. Both numbers were tuned for a 60 Hz frame and read as "invisible" in
the bench, where the camera does not move.

| | bake cycle | moving layer | prefilter | sharp age |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| before | 32 ms, 1 face/frame | 16 ms | 6 frames | 24-30 ms |
| after | 16 ms, 2 faces/frame min | 8 ms | 5 frames | 12-16 ms |

`refl` goes from 0.20 to 0.37 ms of a 3.6 ms frame and the frame time does not move
(3.63 -> 3.58), because the capture is draw bound on the GPU and the extra faces
fill time the frame already spends waiting. That is the whole trade: freshness is
bought in faces, and faces are cheap in wall clock but they are not free, so the
minimum of two exists to keep the age under a frame-time-and-a-half rather than to
make the cube perfect.

Rough mips stay a few frames behind the sharp ones. That is deliberate: a GGX lobe
that is 20 degrees wide hides a 20 ms trail, while a mirror at `lod` 0 does not.

### 8.7 The capture drew pass 0 only: surfaces missing from the cube

`draw_instance` renders a mesh in two passes, split by material flags: pass 0 is
opaque, pass 1 is every section the main view blends (v4 glass with
`0x40000000` and `material_type & 4`; v3 sections with both `alphaOpacity` and
`0x100`). The main world loop runs both passes, in order, so a storefront or a
wall panel with an alpha flag is drawn on top of the opaque world it belongs to.

The reflection capture called `draw_instance(..., pass 0)` and nothing else. So
in the cube those sections did not exist at all. On a building whose walls sit in
the blended pass, the frame, the trim and the roof were all there while the panel
between them was a hole, and the city behind the building showed through it --
which reads as "the garage is see-through in the mirror" rather than "the garage
is missing", because enough of the building is opaque to look present.

The capture now collects the objects it drew and replays pass 1 after the prop
